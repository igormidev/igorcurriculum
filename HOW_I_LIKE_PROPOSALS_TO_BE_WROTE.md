# How I Like Proposals To Be Written

This file defines exactly how my Upwork proposals should be written.

The goal is not to sound fancy. The goal is to maximize reply rate, interview rate, and trust.

Write proposals like a sharp senior engineer who understands the client's problem, speaks clearly, and immediately feels relevant.

## Main Objective

Every proposal should aim to do these things:

- Prove that I actually read the job post
- Make the client feel the proposal was written for them, not mass-sent
- Quickly show why I am a strong match
- Reduce perceived risk
- Make the next step feel easy

The proposal should try to win the reply, not force the whole sale in one message.

## Conversion Principles Behind This Style

These writing preferences are intentional.

- Strong first lines matter because clients often decide whether to open the full proposal based on the beginning
- High-signal personalization works better than generic personalization
- Client-focused language works better than self-focused pitching
- Specific proof converts better than vague confidence
- One low-friction CTA works better than multiple asks competing with each other
- Concise proposals usually perform better than long biographies

## Core Identity To Preserve

The proposal should consistently position me as:

- A Dart / Flutter specialist first
- A senior engineer who cares about product quality, maintainability, and smooth UX
- A freelancer who is reliable, direct, and easy to work with
- A Top Rated Upwork freelancer with 100% Job Success
- Someone who prioritizes the client experience

Do not dilute this positioning unless the job clearly requires a different emphasis.

## Non-Negotiable Personalization Rules

- Every proposal must be tailored to the specific job post
- The first lines must reflect the actual deliverable, pain point, or technical requirement from the job
- If the client's name is available, use it in the first line
- If the client's name is available, start with exactly this structure:

```text
Glad to e-meet you [NAME].
```

- If the client's name is not available, do not invent it
- If the client's name is not available, open with a clean professional line like:

```text
Hello,
```

or

```text
Hi there,
```

- Personalization must be high-signal, not superficial
- Reference what the client actually needs: app type, bug type, feature, performance issue, architecture concern, launch goal, payment flow, state management issue, backend stack, or timeline
- If the client mentions Flutter, Dart, Firebase, Supabase, Serverpod, performance, payments, clean architecture, refactor, app store release, or long-term work, mirror that back naturally
- If the job contains screening questions, treat those as the client's highest priorities and make sure the proposal supports those answers

## Tone And Voice

The tone should be:

- Professional
- Warm
- Confident
- Clear
- Direct
- Calm
- Senior

The tone should not be:

- Desperate
- Overly salesy
- Pushy
- Corporate and buzzword-heavy
- Robotic
- Overly casual
- Overly excited

The writing should feel like a real specialist who knows what he is doing and does not need to oversell.

## Writing Style

- Keep the language simple and strong
- Use short to medium-length sentences
- Prioritize clarity over flourish
- Prefer concrete wording over vague claims
- Use more client-focused language than self-focused language
- Talk more about `your app`, `your users`, `your release`, `your architecture`, `your team`, and `your goal` than about myself
- Avoid jargon unless the client is clearly technical and the jargon helps
- Do not use emoji in proposals
- Do not use cheesy formatting
- Do not use too many exclamation marks
- Do not sound like an agency unless the job explicitly expects an agency

## Pacing And Length

- Keep proposals concise
- Most proposals should stay in the 180 to 300 word range
- Only go longer if the project is complex and the extra detail is clearly useful
- The first two sentences matter the most because clients often see only the start before opening the full proposal
- Get to relevance quickly
- Do not spend the first paragraph introducing myself in generic terms

## What The First Paragraph Must Do

The first paragraph should do three things fast:

- Greet the client
- Show that I understood the job
- Make the client immediately feel I am relevant

Bad first paragraph:

- Generic greeting
- Generic interest statement
- No mention of the actual project

Good first paragraph:

- Personalized opening
- Restates the real problem in my own words
- Signals relevant fit right away

## Preferred Proposal Structure

Use this structure by default:

### 1. Personalized opening

- If name is known: `Glad to e-meet you [NAME].`
- Then immediately reference the actual project need

Example direction:

- `Glad to e-meet you Sarah. You need a Flutter developer to clean up a janky feed, tighten rebuild behavior, and ship new social features without making the codebase harder to maintain.`

### 2. Understanding of the client's need

In one short paragraph:

- Restate the problem
- Show I understood the constraints
- Show I understand the outcome they want, not just the task list

### 3. Relevant proof

Add only the proof that matches the job.

Do not dump my entire background.

Choose 1 to 3 proof points maximum, such as:

- Top Rated on Upwork with 100% Job Success
- Senior Flutter work on an app with 800 thousand users
- Tech lead experience on banking and fleet payment apps
- Health app work with video and chat features
- Real SaaS products shipped end-to-end
- Strong public Flutter/Dart package work
- Backend range with Serverpod, Firebase, or Supabase
- Performance-first mindset
- Package-based architecture / monorepo experience

### 4. Short execution angle

Add a concise explanation of how I would approach the work.

This should be specific enough to sound real, but not so detailed that it becomes unpaid consulting.

Good examples:

- I would first isolate the rebuild hotspots and trace frame drops before touching the UI layer
- I would structure the new feature behind clean boundaries so the project stays maintainable after release
- I would stabilize the payment flow first, then harden edge cases, then finalize the UX

### 5. One low-friction CTA

Use only one CTA.

The default CTA should be low-pressure and easy to answer.

Best default style:

- ask for interest
- offer a short plan
- offer to review the scope
- offer to point out risks or the cleanest implementation path

Good CTA examples:

- `If this sounds aligned, I can send you a short implementation plan for this.`
- `If helpful, I can outline the cleanest technical approach and the main risks I would watch for.`
- `If you'd like, I can break this down into the first practical milestones here on Upwork.`

Only ask for a call immediately if:

- the client explicitly asks for a call
- the project is clearly high-intent and already close to interview stage
- the job post suggests they want fast scheduling

If the proposal is already warm and the client is clearly ready, a more direct CTA is okay:

- `If you'd like, we can discuss this on Upwork messages and set a time that works for you.`

## Important CTA Rule

Do not stack multiple asks in the same proposal.

Avoid endings like:

- `Let me know if you want a call, and I can send examples, and I can estimate cost, and I can answer your questions`

Instead, choose one next step only.

## Social Proof And Risk Reduction

Whenever possible, reduce risk with one compact line of trust-building proof.

Examples:

- `Relevant background: Top Rated on Upwork, 100% Job Success, and senior Flutter delivery on a large production app.`
- `Relevant background: I have already shipped payment-heavy and architecture-sensitive Flutter apps in production.`
- `Relevant background: I have real product experience beyond task work, including complete SaaS systems built end-to-end.`

Do not overdo this. One compact proof line is usually enough.

## Links And Work Samples

Include links only when they are strongly relevant to the job.

- Prefer one highly relevant link over many links
- Use my curriculum site, GitHub, package links, or SaaS links only when they directly support the proposal
- If the job is about Flutter packages, reusable UI, text rendering, pricing screens, or editor-like components, linking a relevant package is useful
- If the job is about full product thinking, architecture, or SaaS delivery, linking the curriculum site or a strong GitHub project can help
- Do not paste a random list of links at the end
- Do not overwhelm the client with portfolio dumping
- If no link materially strengthens the proposal, leave links out

## Proposal Content Priorities

Prioritize proposal space in this order:

1. The client's actual need
2. Why I am relevant to that need
3. The safest / cleanest path to results
4. A low-friction next step
5. Extra background only if necessary

## What To Highlight Depending On The Job

Use the proof that best matches the job type.

### If the job is about Flutter feature delivery

Highlight:

- Dart / Flutter specialist
- senior production experience
- ability to ship polished, stable features
- client communication and clean delivery

### If the job is about performance, lag, rebuilds, or stability

Highlight:

- performance-first mindset
- rebuild control
- widget lifecycle discipline
- render pipeline awareness
- DevTools and profiling
- experience shipping smooth apps under real usage

### If the job is about architecture, refactor, or maintainability

Highlight:

- tech lead experience
- clean architecture when useful
- modular codebases
- package-based monorepos
- internal packages
- code that is easy to maintain, modify, and scale

### If the job is about fintech, payments, or transaction flows

Highlight:

- banking app tech lead experience
- fleet payment app experience
- Brazilian Pix integration
- reliability and edge-case awareness

### If the job is about social, media, video, or chat

Highlight:

- 800-thousand-user social app experience
- YouTube Shorts integration
- video and chat feature work in health products
- UX and engagement sensitivity

### If the job is about backend or full-stack Dart

Highlight:

- Serverpod experience
- Firebase / Supabase range
- shelf / Aqueduct / dart_frog / gRPC familiarity
- security mindset
- Row Level Security experience where relevant

### If the job is about full product ownership or SaaS

Highlight:

- ZenScrap
- Mustache Hub
- ability to ship end-to-end
- architecture, product thinking, analytics, billing, backend, and frontend ownership

### If the job is about reusable UI, packages, or tooling

Highlight:

- public package work
- package design thinking
- `babel_text`, `pricing_page`, `youtube_shorts`, `cursor_autocomplete_options`, and other relevant packages

### If the job is about AI features or AI-assisted engineering

Highlight:

- AI systems mindset
- MCP / agent orchestration
- context engineering
- LangChain / RAG familiarity
- internal tooling and workflow design

Only bring up AI if it is actually relevant to the job.

## Questions Inside The Proposal

Questions can help, but they must be useful.

- Ask 0 to 2 questions maximum
- Ask only questions that meaningfully affect scope, architecture, risk, or delivery
- Do not ask questions already answered in the job post
- Do not overload the client with a questionnaire
- If the client included screening questions, prioritize those instead of adding extra questions in the cover letter

Good question examples:

- `Is this already in production, or is it still pre-release?`
- `Are you looking for a quick stabilization pass first, or a deeper refactor with long-term maintainability in mind?`

Bad question examples:

- `Can you tell me more?`
- `Do you have a budget?`
- `When do you want to start?`

unless the job post makes that genuinely necessary.

## What Must Never Appear

Never write proposals that contain:

- `To whom it may concern`
- `Dear Sir/Madam`
- `I am the best candidate`
- `I can do anything`
- `Kindly`
- `Please give me a chance`
- `I need this job`
- `I am new but very passionate`
- `I read your post and I am interested`
- `I have gone through your requirements`
- `We are a team of experts`
- fake urgency
- exaggerated promises
- unsupported claims
- made-up experience
- copied generic filler
- obvious AI-sounding phrasing

Do not mention:

- that this proposal was generated by AI
- that I am applying automatically
- that I am sending many proposals

## What To Avoid Stylistically

- Do not overuse bullets inside the actual proposal
- Do not write a wall of text
- Do not front-load the proposal with biography
- Do not turn it into a portfolio dump
- Do not use buzzword soup like `synergy`, `innovative solutions`, `dynamic team player`, `cutting-edge`, or `results-driven professional`
- Do not overuse `I`, `my`, and `we`
- Do not sound needy
- Do not sound fake-friendly

## Preferred Level Of Directness

Be direct, but not aggressive.

Good:

- clear
- relevant
- self-assured
- honest about the match

Bad:

- arrogant
- pushy
- overconfident without proof

The client should feel:

- this person understands my project
- this person has done similar work
- this person will be easy to work with
- this person can probably solve this cleanly

## Proposal Template

Use this as the default skeleton and adapt it to the job:

```text
Glad to e-meet you [NAME].

You need [brief restatement of the real project need, pain point, or outcome]. Based on your post, the important part here is [constraint / risk / goal], not just [surface task].

I’m a Dart / Flutter specialist, and this is the kind of work I’m strongest at. [Add 1-3 highly relevant proof points only.] [Optional: one short line about how I would approach the work.]

If this sounds aligned, I can [single low-friction next step].
```

If the client's name is unknown, use:

```text
Hello,

You need [brief restatement of the real project need, pain point, or outcome]. Based on your post, the important part here is [constraint / risk / goal], not just [surface task].

I’m a Dart / Flutter specialist, and this is the kind of work I’m strongest at. [Add 1-3 highly relevant proof points only.] [Optional: one short line about how I would approach the work.]

If this sounds aligned, I can [single low-friction next step].
```

## Better Proposal Examples

### Example: Flutter performance and refactor job

```text
Glad to e-meet you [NAME].

You need more than someone to patch a few Flutter screens. From your post, the real need is to improve performance and stabilize the codebase so new features can be added without more UI lag and maintenance pain.

I’m a Dart / Flutter specialist with senior production experience, including work on a Flutter app with 800 thousand users. I’m especially strong in rebuild control, rendering discipline, and structuring code so the app stays smooth and maintainable as it grows.

If helpful, I can outline the first technical pass I would make to isolate the biggest performance risks and cleanly prioritize the fixes.
```

### Example: Flutter fintech / payment flow job

```text
Glad to e-meet you [NAME].

You’re not just looking for Flutter help here. You need someone who can ship a payment-sensitive flow safely, handle edge cases carefully, and keep the UX clear for end users.

I’m a Dart / Flutter specialist and have already worked on banking and fleet payment products in production, including Pix integration work. I tend to be especially useful when reliability, maintainability, and smooth delivery all matter at the same time.

If this sounds aligned, I can break this into the cleanest first milestones and point out the main implementation risks I would watch for.
```

### Example: Long-term Flutter product role

```text
Glad to e-meet you [NAME].

You’re looking for someone who can contribute beyond isolated tickets and help your Flutter product move forward in a stable, maintainable way. That long-term fit matters as much as the immediate feature work.

I’m a Top Rated Upwork freelancer with 100% Job Success, and I sell myself first as a Dart / Flutter specialist. I’ve shipped senior Flutter work in production, led architecture-sensitive projects, and I care a lot about communication, clean code, and user experience.

If you’d like, I can send a short note on how I usually work inside long-term client projects and how I keep delivery predictable.
```

## Final Review Checklist

Before a proposal is sent, make sure all of this is true:

- It clearly matches the job post
- It sounds personalized
- The opening is strong enough to earn the click
- The client's name is used if available
- It starts with `Glad to e-meet you [NAME].` when the name is known
- The proposal is concise
- It contains relevant proof, not a biography dump
- It sounds like a Dart / Flutter specialist
- It contains only one CTA
- The CTA is low-friction unless the situation is already warm
- The grammar is clean
- The message does not sound templated
- It does not contain banned phrases

## Final Preference Summary

If there is one thing to optimize for, optimize for this:

Make the client feel that I understood exactly what they need, that I am unusually relevant to it, and that replying to me will be easy and low-risk.
